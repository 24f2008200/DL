# Deep Learning: Algorithm Comparison Tables

Compiled from the course slides (Lectures 2–13). Notation: $\eta$ learning rate, $\beta$ momentum decay, $\nabla w_t$ gradient at step $t$, $\epsilon$ small constant.

## A. Learning and optimization algorithms

| **Algorithm**                           | **Key idea**                                                                                                         | **Formula**                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | **Strengths**                                                                                                                                                                                                    | **Weaknesses**                                                                                                                                                               |
|:----------------------------------------|:---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Perceptron Learning Algorithm (PLA)** | Fix mistakes one point at a time: add misclassified positives to $w$, subtract misclassified negatives.              | $\mathbf{x}\in P,\ w^{T}x<0\Rightarrow w\leftarrow w+x$<br>$\mathbf{x}\in N,\ w^{T}x\ge0\Rightarrow w\leftarrow w-x$                                                                                                                   | Very simple. Guaranteed to finish after finitely many updates if the data is linearly separable.                                                                                                                 | Only linearly separable data (fails on XOR). Never converges otherwise. No probabilistic output.                                                                             |
| **Backpropagation**                     | Chain rule applied layer by layer, backwards, to get every gradient.                                                 | $\nabla_{a_L}L=\hat y-e_\ell$ (softmax + cross-entropy) <br>$\nabla_{h_{k-1}}L=W_k^{T} \nabla_{a_k}L$<br>$\nabla_{a_{k-1}}L=\nabla_{h_{k-1}}L\odot g'(a_{k-1})$<br>$\nabla_{W_k}L=\nabla_{a_k}L\,h_{k-1}^{T}$<br>$\nabla_{b_k}L=\nabla_{a_k}L$    | Exact gradients of all parameters from one forward and one backward pass. Works for any stack of differentiable layers.                                                                                          | Needs differentiable activations. With sigmoid/tanh the factor $g'$ is small (at most 0.25 for sigmoid), so gradients shrink layer by layer (vanishing gradients).           |
| **Batch gradient descent**              | Move opposite the gradient of the full-data loss (from the Taylor expansion, $u=-\nabla L$ is the steepest descent). | $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\,\nabla L(\theta_t)$                                                                                                                                                                                   | Exact gradient; loss decreases each step for small $\eta$. Simple, few hyperparameters.                                                                                                                          | One update per pass over all $N$ points. Tiny gradient on gentle slopes, so progress is very slow there. One global $\eta$ for every parameter.                              |
| **Momentum**                            | Keep an exponentially weighted history of gradients and move along it.                                               | $u_t=\beta u_{t-1}+\nabla w_t$<br>$w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta\,u_t$                                                                                                                                                                              | Larger steps on gentle slopes and plateaus. Faster than plain GD (slide example: error $10^{-5}$ vs. $0.16$ after 50 iterations).                                                                                | Overshoots; oscillates with U-turns across a valley. Adds hyperparameter $\beta$.                                                                                            |
| **Nesterov accelerated gradient (NAG)** | “Look before you leap”: evaluate the gradient at the look-ahead point, then step.                                    | $u_t=\beta u_{t-1}+\nabla\bigl(w_t-\beta u_{t-1}\bigr)$<br>$w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta\,u_t$                                                                                                                                                     | Smaller oscillations and shorter U-turns than momentum; corrects course sooner.                                                                                                                                  | Same $\beta$ and global $\eta$ as momentum. Needs the gradient at a shifted point (different from the current parameters).                                                   |
| **Stochastic GD (SGD)**                 | Update after each single example, using its gradient as an estimate of the full gradient.                            | $w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta\,\nabla w_t$ with $\nabla w_t$ from one data point ($N$ steps per epoch)                                                                                                                                         | Very fast updates; cheap per step; works on huge datasets.                                                                                                                                                       | Noisy estimate (like estimating $P(\text{heads})$ from one toss). Many oscillations; no guarantee each step lowers the loss.                                                 |
| **Mini-batch GD**                       | Use $B$ points per update: compromise between batch and SGD.                                                         | Same rule with the gradient averaged over $B$ points ($N/B$ steps per epoch)                                                                                                                                                       | Better estimate than SGD (fewer oscillations, e.g. $B=25$); bigger $B$ means more accurate. Still many updates per epoch.                                                                                        | Gradient is still approximate, so oscillations never vanish. Batch size $B$ must be chosen.                                                                                  |
| **Line search**                         | Choose $\eta$ every step by trying several values and keeping the best.                                              | Try $\eta\in\{0.1,0.5,1,2,10\}$, keep the one with lowest loss                                                                                                                                                                     | Adapts $\eta$ automatically; converges faster than fixed-$\eta$ GD.                                                                                                                                              | Several extra loss evaluations per step. Oscillation behaviour differs from momentum/NAG.                                                                                    |
| **AdaGrad**                             | Per-parameter learning rate: divide by the root of the accumulated squared gradients.                                | $v_t=v_{t-1}+(\nabla w_t)^2$<br>$w_{t+1}=w_t-\dfrac{\eta}{\sqrt{v_t+\epsilon}}\,\nabla w_t$                                                                                                                                            | Sparse features (rarely updated) keep a large rate; frequent ones (e.g. bias) get a small rate. No manual per-parameter tuning.                                                                                  | $v_t$ only grows, so the effective rate decays monotonically and updates become tiny near the minimum. Cannot raise $\eta$ later; large early gradients keep it low forever. |
| **RMSProp**                             | Replace AdaGrad’s sum by an exponential moving average so old gradients are forgotten.                               | $v_t=\beta v_{t-1}+(1-\beta)(\nabla w_t)^2$<br>$w_{t+1}=w_t-\dfrac{\eta}{\sqrt{v_t+\epsilon}}\,\nabla w_t$                                                                                                                             | Rate can rise or fall, so it does not die like AdaGrad. Per-parameter rates.                                                                                                                                     | Still sensitive to the initial $\eta_0$. A constant rate near the minimum can oscillate. No momentum.                                                                        |
| **AdaDelta**                            | Like RMSProp but the rate numerator is a running average of past *updates*, removing the need for $\eta_0$.          | $\Delta w_t=-\dfrac{\sqrt{u_{t-1}+\epsilon}}{\sqrt{v_t+\epsilon}}\,\nabla w_t$,  $w_{t+1}=w_t+\Delta w_t$<br>$u_t=\beta u_{t-1}+(1-\beta)(\Delta w_t)^2$                                                                               | No initial learning rate to tune. Per-parameter, adaptive.                                                                                                                                                       | Two running averages ($v$ and $u$) must be kept. No momentum-style gradient history.                                                                                         |
| **Adam**                                | RMSProp-style adaptive rate plus a cumulative gradient history (momentum), with bias correction.                     | $m_t=\beta_1m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_1)\nabla w_t$,  $\hat m_t=\dfrac{m_t}{1-\beta_1^t}$<br>$v_t=\beta_2v_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)(\nabla w_t)^2$,  $\hat v_t=\dfrac{v_t}{1-\beta_2^t}$<br>$w_{t+1}=w_t-\dfrac{\eta}{\sqrt{\hat v_t+\epsilon}}\,\hat m_t$ | Fast and robust; the practical default (typical $\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.999$, $\epsilon=10^{-8}$). Bias correction avoids huge early steps (example: $1/\sqrt{v_0}\approx316$ uncorrected vs. $10$ corrected). | May fail to converge in some cases and often generalizes worse than SGD with momentum and a schedule. Three hyperparameters.                                                 |
| **AdaMax**                              | Adam with the max (infinity) norm instead of the $L_2$ norm for $v_t$.                                               | $v_t=\max\bigl(\beta_2v_{t-1},\lvert\nabla w_t\rvert\bigr)$<br>$w_{t+1}=w_t-\dfrac{\eta}{v_t+\epsilon}\,\hat m_t$                                                                                                                      | $v_t$ needs no bias correction; stable scale.                                                                                                                                                                    | $\hat m_t$ still needs bias correction. Not a uniform improvement over Adam.                                                                                                 |
| **NAdam**                               | Adam with Nesterov look-ahead; rewritten so the look-ahead momentum is applied only once per step.                   | $g_{t+1}=\nabla w_t$,  $m_{t+1}=\beta m_t+g_{t+1}$<br>$w_{t+1}=w_t-(\beta m_{t+1}+g_{t+1})$  ($\eta=1$ as drawn on the slides)                                                                                                         | Keeps Adam’s adaptivity and adds NAG’s earlier course correction.                                                                                                                                                | Same hyperparameters as Adam; a more involved update rule.                                                                                                                   |

## B. Learning-rate schedules

| **Schedule**                            | **Key idea**                                                                                                          | **Formula**                                                                                                                             | **Strengths**                                                 | **Weaknesses**                                                                  |
|:----------------------------------------|:----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Step decay**                          | Cut $\eta$ at fixed points or when validation error stops improving.                                                  | Halve $\eta$ every 5 epochs, or when validation error rises vs. the previous epoch                                                      | Simple; the validation-based version reacts to training.      | Heuristic; interval and factor are hand-set.                                    |
| **Exponential decay**                   | Smooth continuous decrease.                                                                                           | $\eta=\eta_0e^{-kt}$                                                                                                                    | Smooth, no abrupt changes.                                    | Extra hyperparameter $k$; once $\eta$ is small it cannot escape saddle regions. |
| **$1/t$ decay**                         | Slower, polynomial-type decrease.                                                                                     | $\eta=\dfrac{\eta_0}{1+kt}$                                                                                                             | Simple.                                                       | Needs $\eta_0$ and $k$.                                                         |
| **Cyclical LR (triangular)**            | Vary $\eta$ cyclically between bounds: saddle points are the real difficulty, and a decayed $\eta$ cannot escape one. | $\eta_t=\eta_{\min}+(\eta_{\max}-\eta_{\min})\max\Bigl(0,1-\bigl\lvert\frac{t}{\mu}-2\lfloor1+\frac{t}{2\mu}\rfloor+1\bigr\rvert\Bigr)$ | Periodic large rates help traverse saddles and plateaus.      | Three extra hyperparameters ($\eta_{\min},\eta_{\max},\mu$).                    |
| **Cosine annealing with warm restarts** | Anneal $\eta$ along a cosine from $\eta_{\max}$ to $\eta_{\min}$ over $T$ steps, then restart.                        | $\eta_t=\eta_{\min}+\dfrac{\eta_{\max}-\eta_{\min}}{2}\Bigl(1+\cos\bigl(\pi\,\frac{t\bmod(T+1)}{T}\bigr)\Bigr)$                         | Smooth decay with periodic restarts.                          | Need to pick $T$, $\eta_{\min}$, $\eta_{\max}$.                                 |
| **Warm-up**                             | Start with a small $\eta$, increase it, then decay.                                                                   | e.g. 4000 warm-up steps in transformers                                                                                                 | Gentle start before the rate is raised; used in transformers. | Another schedule hyperparameter.                                                |

## C. Regularization and generalization techniques

| **Technique**                           | **Key idea**                                                                                          | **Formula / mechanism**                                                                                                                                    | **Strengths**                                                                                       | **Weaknesses**                                                                                                  |
|:----------------------------------------|:------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **$L_2$ regularization (weight decay)** | Penalize large weights to limit model complexity (trade a little bias for less variance).             | $\tilde L=L+\frac{\alpha}{2}\lVert w\rVert^2$<br>$w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta\nabla L-\eta\alpha w_t$each eigen-direction scaled by $\dfrac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_i+\alpha}$ | Simple. Keeps weights only in important directions (large $\lambda_i$); fewer effective parameters. | Hyperparameter $\alpha$; too large causes underfitting (high bias).                                             |
| **$L_1$ regularization**                | Penalty on absolute weights.                                                                          | Pushes most weights to exactly 0 (named in the lecture)                                                                                                    | Sparse weight vectors.                                                                              | Extra hyperparameter; slides give no derivation.                                                                |
| **Early stopping**                      | Track validation error; stop when it stops improving for $p$ steps and return the best earlier model. | Equivalent to $L_2$ when $(I-\eta\Lambda)^t=(\Lambda+\alpha I)^{-1}\alpha$                                                                                 | Very effective, most widely used; easy; can combine with $L_2$.                                     | Needs validation data, a patience $p$, and a stored copy of the best model.                                     |
| **Dataset augmentation**                | Create more training data by transforming existing data (rotate, shift, …).                           | Same label, transformed input                                                                                                                              | Works very well for images and speech; reduces overfitting.                                         | Not obvious for all tasks; transformations are domain-specific.                                                 |
| **Noise on inputs**                     | Add Gaussian noise to inputs.                                                                         | $\mathbb{E}[(\tilde y-y)^2]=\mathbb{E}[(\hat y-y)^2]+\sigma^2\sum w_i^2$ (acts as $L_2$)                                                                   | Cheap form of $L_2$.                                                                                | Needs $\sigma$.                                                                                                 |
| **Label smoothing (noise on outputs)**  | Do not fully trust labels: soft targets.                                                              | True class $1-\epsilon$, others $\epsilon/(k-1)$; cross-entropy $-\sum p_i\log q_i$                                                                        | Robust to wrong labels; mild regularization.                                                        | Needs $\epsilon$.                                                                                               |
| **Bagging / ensembles**                 | Train $k$ models on bootstrap samples and average or vote.                                            | $\text{mse}=\frac1kV+\frac{k-1}{k}C$                                                                                                                       | Reduces variance; on average never worse than a member; independent errors give $V/k$.              | Costs $k$ trainings; no gain when errors are perfectly correlated ($C=V$).                                      |
| **Dropout**                             | Randomly drop nodes during training: effectively $2^n$ thinned networks sharing weights.              | Retain prob. $p$ ($0.5$ hidden, $0.8$ input); at test time use the full network scaled by $p$                                                              | Cheap approximate ensemble; prevents co-adaptation, forces redundancy.                              | Retain probability must be chosen; training and test networks differ (scaling needed).                          |
| **Parameter sharing / tying**           | Reuse the same weights in several places.                                                             | CNN filters; encoder–decoder tied weights                                                                                                                  | Far fewer parameters, built-in regularization.                                                      | Assumes the same pattern applies everywhere.                                                                    |
| **Unsupervised pre-training**           | Train each layer as an autoencoder, then fine-tune the whole net with the supervised loss.            | $\min\frac1m\sum_i\sum_j(\hat x_{ij}-x_{ij})^2$ layer by layer, then supervised objective                                                                  | Better initialization and an implicit regularizer ($\Omega(\theta)$ = reconstruction term).         | Extra training stage; progress since came from better optimizers, regularizers, activations and initialization. |

## D. Neuron models and network architectures

| **Model**                       | **Key idea**                                                                                                                        | **Formula / key fact**                                                                                                    | **Strengths**                                                                                                                  | **Weaknesses**                                                                                                                  |
|:--------------------------------|:------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **McCulloch–Pitts neuron**      | Boolean inputs, fixed weights, hand-set threshold.                                                                                  | $y=1$ iff $\sum x_i\ge\theta$ (inhibitory input $=1\Rightarrow y=0$)                                                      | Implements AND, OR, NOR, NOT, tautology.                                                                                       | No learning; boolean only; linearly separable functions only.                                                                   |
| **Perceptron**                  | Real inputs, learnable weights and bias.                                                                                            | $y=1$ iff $\sum_{i=0}^{n}w_ix_i\ge0$, $w_0=-\theta$                                                                       | Learns weights (PLA). A network with $2^n$ hidden units represents any boolean function.                                       | Abrupt jump at the threshold (0.49 vs. 0.51); not differentiable; single unit cannot do XOR; $2^n$ hidden units is exponential. |
| **Sigmoid neuron**              | Smooth version of the perceptron, output read as a probability.                                                                     | $y=\dfrac{1}{1+e^{-(w^{T}x+b)}}$,  $\sigma'=\sigma(1-\sigma)$                                                             | Differentiable, so gradient descent applies; with one hidden layer and enough neurons it approximates any continuous function. | Saturates (vanishing gradient); output not zero-centred; one neuron has a single linear boundary.                               |
| **CNN (convolution + pooling)** | Sparse connectivity and weight sharing: the same small kernel slides over the input.                                                | $W_2=\dfrac{W_1-F+2P}{S}+1$,  $D_2=K$; params $=F\cdot F\cdot D_1\cdot K$                                                 | Far fewer parameters than a fully connected net; reuses the same pattern detector at every location.                           | Local receptive field: distant pixels interact only through depth; many hyperparameters ($F,S,P,K$).                            |
| **AlexNet (2012)**              | layers, ReLU, large first filter.                                                                                                   | $227\times227\times3$, 96 filters $11\times11$ stride 4 $\to55\times55\times96$; 27.55M params; top-5 error 16.4%         | First deep net to win ImageNet by a large margin.                                                                              | Coarse first layer ($11\times11$, stride 4), improved by ZFNet.                                                                 |
| **ZFNet (2013)**                | Tune AlexNet: smaller first filter, more filters later.                                                                             | First filter $7\times7$; 11.7% error                                                                                      | Lower error with the same 8-layer layout.                                                                                      | Same overall design and cost as AlexNet (about 1.45M more params).                                                              |
| **VGG (2014)**                  | Only $3\times3$ kernels, much deeper (19 layers).                                                                                   | About 16M params in non-FC layers, about 122M in FC layers; 7.3% error                                                    | Simple, uniform design; strong accuracy.                                                                                       | Very many parameters, mostly in the first FC layer ($\approx102$M).                                                             |
| **GoogLeNet (2014)**            | Inception module: parallel $1\times1$, $3\times3$, $5\times5$ and pooling branches, concatenated; $1\times1$ convs cut depth first. | Cost per output drops from $O(F^2D)$ to $O(F^2D_1)$; global average pooling avoids a $49$M-parameter FC layer; 6.7% error | layers with far fewer parameters and computation than VGG.                                                                     | Complex hand-designed module.                                                                                                   |
| **ResNet (2015)**               | Residual block with identity skip connection: learn $F(x)$, output $H(x)=F(x)+x$.                                                   | layers; 3.57% error; batch norm, SGD + momentum 0.9, $\eta=0.1$ divided by 10 on plateau                                  | Very deep nets train well; plain deep nets got higher error because extra layers could not even learn identity.                | Needs the training recipe (batch norm, initialization, schedule); very deep.                                                    |

## E. Which one to pick (rules of thumb, mostly from the slides)

- **Default optimizer:** Adam (or NAdam); for sequence generation $\eta=0.001$ or $0.0001$. SGD with momentum or NAG plus an annealing schedule also works well and often generalizes better.

- **Sparse features:** an adaptive per-parameter method (AdaGrad, RMSProp, Adam), not plain GD.

- **Large datasets:** mini-batch (not full batch, not single-point SGD).

- **Regularize deep nets:** early stopping $+$ dropout (plus augmentation for images); $L_2$ as needed.

- **Activations:** ReLU first (try Leaky ReLU/ELU/Maxout); avoid sigmoid; GELU in transformers.
